Accounting Management

Complete Guide to Financial Record Keeping, Transactions, and Reporting

Overview

The Accounting Management module provides comprehensive financial management capabilities including general ledger maintenance, accounts receivable/payable management, financial reporting, and regulatory compliance. Users can manage chart of accounts, record journal entries, track cash flow, generate financial statements, and maintain audit trails.

This module integrates with sales, purchasing, inventory, and payroll systems to provide complete financial visibility and control.

Account Classification

Account Type
Primary classification determining account behavior:
  • Assets: Cash, receivables, inventory, equipment, investments
  • Liabilities: Payables, loans, accrued expenses, deferred revenue
  • Equity: Capital, retained earnings, dividends
  • Revenue: Sales, service income, investment income
  • Expenses: Cost of goods sold, operating expenses, taxes
Account Number
Unique numeric identifier following standard coding conventions:
  • 1xxx - Assets (1100-Cash, 1200-Receivables, 1300-Inventory)
  • 2xxx - Liabilities (2100-Payables, 2200-Accruals, 2300-Long-term Debt)
  • 3xxx - Equity (3100-Capital, 3200-Retained Earnings)
  • 4xxx - Revenue (4100-Sales, 4200-Services, 4300-Other Income)
  • 5xxx - Expenses (5100-COGS, 5200-Operating, 5300-Admin)
Account Name
Clear, descriptive name for the account. Should be specific enough for easy identification but concise for reporting.
Normal Balance
Natural balance side for the account:
  • Debit: Assets, Expenses
  • Credit: Liabilities, Equity, Revenue

Account Properties

Active/Inactive
Control whether account is available for new transactions. Inactive accounts retain historical data but cannot receive new entries.
Bank Account
Designate if this is a bank account for cash management and bank reconciliation processes.
Tax Account
Specify if account is used for tax calculations and reporting. Links to tax rates and compliance requirements.
Reconciliation Required
Require monthly reconciliation for balance accuracy (typically bank accounts and credit card accounts).
💡 Tip:
Follow standard chart of accounts numbering to ensure consistency with accounting software and reporting standards.

Journal Entries and Transactions

Recording Journal Entries

  1. Navigate to Accounting → Journal Entries → Create Entry.
    [Screenshot: Journal entry creation interface]
  2. Enter transaction header information.
    [Screenshot: Journal entry header form]
  3. Add debit and credit line items.
  4. Verify that total debits equal total credits.
  5. Click Post to record the transaction.

Journal Entry Header

Entry Date
Transaction date for the journal entry. Must be within an open accounting period.
Reference Number
Unique identifier for the journal entry. Auto-generated but can be customized for specific numbering schemes.
Description
Brief explanation of the transaction purpose. Should provide clear context for future reference and audit purposes.
Source Document
Reference to supporting documentation (invoice number, receipt, contract reference). Essential for audit trails.

Journal Entry Line Items

Account
Chart of accounts selection for this line item. Must be an active account appropriate for the transaction type.
Debit Amount
Debit value for accounts with normal debit balance (assets, expenses) or to decrease credit balance accounts.
Credit Amount
Credit value for accounts with normal credit balance (liabilities, equity, revenue) or to decrease debit balance accounts.
Line Description
Specific details for this line item. Can be more detailed than the journal entry header description.
Project/Department
Optional allocation to specific projects, departments, or cost centers for detailed tracking and reporting.
⚠️ Important:
All journal entries must balance (total debits = total credits) before they can be posted. The system will prevent posting unbalanced entries.

Accounts Receivable Management

Customer Account Setup

Customer Information
Complete contact details, billing addresses, and communication preferences for accurate invoicing and collections.
Credit Terms
Standard payment terms for the customer:
  • Net 15: Payment due 15 days after invoice
  • Net 30: Payment due 30 days after invoice
  • 2/10 Net 30: 2% discount if paid within 10 days, otherwise due in 30
  • COD: Cash on delivery
  • Prepaid: Payment required before delivery
Credit Limit
Maximum outstanding balance allowed for the customer. System can warn or block new sales when limit is exceeded.

Invoice Processing

  1. Invoice automatically creates accounts receivable entry when posted.
  2. Customer balance increases by invoice amount.
  3. Revenue is recognized based on accounting method (cash or accrual).
  4. Payment terms determine due date and aging calculations.

Payment Processing

Payment Application
Record customer payments and apply to specific invoices. Can handle partial payments and payment allocation across multiple invoices.
Bank Reconciliation
Match recorded payments with bank deposits to ensure accuracy and identify discrepancies.
Cash Discounts
Apply early payment discounts when customers pay within discount terms. Automatically calculate discount amounts.

Accounts Payable Management

Vendor Setup

Vendor Information
Complete vendor details including remittance addresses, tax identification numbers, and payment preferences.
Payment Terms
Standard terms received from vendors. Important for cash flow planning and discount opportunities.
1099 Reporting
Flag vendors requiring 1099 reporting for tax compliance. Track total payments for annual reporting.

Financial Reporting

Standard Financial Statements

Report Purpose Key Information
Balance Sheet Financial position at specific date Assets, liabilities, equity balances
Income Statement Revenue and expenses for period Sales, cost of goods sold, operating expenses, net income
Cash Flow Statement Cash receipts and disbursements Operating, investing, financing activities
Statement of Equity Changes in ownership equity Capital contributions, retained earnings, distributions

Management Reports

Aged Receivables
Outstanding customer balances by aging period (current, 30, 60, 90+ days). Critical for collections management.
Aged Payables
Outstanding vendor balances by due date. Helps manage cash flow and vendor relationships.
Budget vs. Actual
Comparison of actual results to budgeted amounts. Identify variances and trends for management analysis.
Departmental P&L
Profit and loss by department or cost center. Enable departmental performance evaluation and accountability.

  3. Choose date range and filtering options.
  4. Generate report in PDF or Excel format.
  5. Schedule automated report distribution for regular reporting cycles.

Period End Procedures

Monthly Closing Checklist

  1. Record Recurring Entries: Depreciation, accruals, prepaid amortization.
    [Screenshot: Recurring entries management]
  2. Bank Reconciliation: Match bank statements to cash accounts.
    [Screenshot: Bank reconciliation interface]
  3. Inventory Adjustments: Update inventory values and record adjustments.
  4. Review Accounts: Analyze unusual balances and resolve discrepancies.
  5. Generate Trial Balance: Verify that all accounts balance before closing.
  6. Close Period: Lock the accounting period to prevent further changes.

Accrual Entries

Revenue Accruals
Record revenue earned but not yet billed. Common for contracts with milestone billing or service periods.
Expense Accruals
Record expenses incurred but not yet billed. Include utilities, professional services, and employee benefits.
Prepaid Adjustments
Amortize prepaid expenses over applicable periods. Common for insurance, software licenses, and rent.
Depreciation
Record monthly depreciation on fixed assets using appropriate methods (straight-line, accelerated).

Tax Management and Compliance

Sales Tax Configuration

Tax Rates
Configure applicable tax rates by jurisdiction (state, county, city). Update rates as regulations change.
Tax Exemptions
Manage customer tax exemptions with supporting documentation. Track exemption numbers and expiration dates.
Product Tax Classes
Classify products by taxability (taxable, exempt, reduced rate). Ensures correct tax calculation on sales.

Tax Reporting

W-2 Forms Annual Report employee wages and tax withholdings
Income Tax Returns Annual Corporate tax filing with supporting schedules

Use Cases & Examples

Service Business Accounting

Professional services firm managing project-based revenue:

  • Time tracking integration: Capture billable hours for revenue recognition
  • Project accounting: Track costs and revenue by client engagement
  • Retainer management: Handle advance payments and draw-downs
  • Expense allocation: Distribute overhead costs across projects
  • Revenue recognition: Percentage of completion or milestone-based
  • WIP reporting: Work-in-progress analysis and billing efficiency
  • Client profitability: Analyze margins by client and service type
Manufacturing Company Accounting

Manufacturing business with complex cost accounting requirements:

  • Inventory costing: Track raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods
  • Cost of goods sold: Calculate based on production and sales
  • Standard costing: Set standard costs and analyze variances
  • Overhead allocation: Distribute manufacturing overhead to products
  • Fixed asset management: Track equipment depreciation and maintenance
  • Multi-location reporting: Consolidate results across manufacturing sites
  • Regulatory compliance: Meet industry-specific accounting requirements
Retail Business Accounting

Retail company managing high-volume transactions and inventory:

  • Point-of-sale integration: Automatic daily sales recording
  • Inventory management: Track stock levels and cost of goods sold
  • Multi-location consolidation: Combine results from multiple stores
  • Sales tax management: Handle complex multi-state tax requirements
  • Seasonal adjustments: Manage inventory markdowns and seasonal trends
  • Vendor rebates: Track and account for supplier rebate programs
  • Gift card accounting: Handle deferred revenue for gift card sales

Best Practices

  • Regular Reconciliation: Perform monthly bank reconciliations and balance sheet account analysis.
  • Supporting Documentation: Maintain complete documentation for all transactions and journal entries.
  • Period End Procedures: Follow consistent month-end and year-end closing procedures.
  • Internal Controls: Implement segregation of duties and approval workflows for financial transactions.
  • Backup and Security: Regular data backups and secure access controls for financial information.
  • Audit Preparation: Maintain organized records and documentation for internal and external audits.
  • Training and Updates: Keep accounting staff trained on system updates and regulatory changes.
Summary

The Accounting Management module provides comprehensive financial management capabilities that support accurate record keeping, regulatory compliance, and strategic business decision making. From daily transaction processing to monthly financial reporting, the system ensures financial integrity while providing the visibility and control needed for successful business operations.
